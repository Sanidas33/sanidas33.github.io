Lost & Found Drinkery

Nicosia, CY · 2014 — 2022 · Head bartender, founding team

Lost & Found Drinkery — the room itself. Photo: via 50 Best Discovery.

From a side-street twenty-seater to #25 in the world.

The brief

A twenty-seat bar tucked off a side street in old Nicosia, opening into a market where cocktail culture meant vodka and Red Bull. No international bar from Cyprus had ever registered on the global stage. The ambition was absurd on paper: build a world-list bar in a city the bar world had never once looked at.

What we did

  • Built the bar program from zero as head bartender and founding-team member: a tight, technique-led repertoire rather than an encyclopedic list
  • Developed prep systems that let a tiny room deliver world-class consistency night after night
  • Trained a team on a simple doctrine — call regulars by name before they reach the stool, and never serve a drink you can’t serve twice
  • Evolved the menu annually for eight years, keeping a small bar interesting to an international audience that had already seen everything

What changed

  • First Cypriot bar ever to make the World’s 50 Best Bars list (2015) — then held a place on the lists for nine consecutive years (2015–2023)
  • Peaked at #25 in the world, 2018
  • Two Tales of the Cocktail Spirited Awards Top 10 nominations in the same year — Best International Cocktail Bar and Best International Bar Team (2018), in a cohort with Manhattan Bar Singapore, the Connaught, and the Savoy’s American Bar
  • Featured in Difford’s Guide, Bargiornale, and Parikiaki

Here Nor There / In Plain Sight

Austin, TX · 2022 — 2023 · Consulting — menu, COGS, training

Here Nor There, Austin. Photo: via 50 Best Discovery.

A national name within twelve months.

The brief

Two rooms at one downtown Austin address — Here Nor There, the hidden-entry, reservation-only speakeasy flagship, and In Plain Sight, its aperitivo-style sister bar. Ambitious drinks, real talent, and the classic growth problem: a menu whose prep load and pour costs hadn’t been engineered to match its ambition.

What we did

  • Audited the running menu across live service — prep efficiency, pour costs, and where the drinks were quietly bleeding margin
  • Fixed COGS drink by drink: re-costed specs, tightened batching, and rebuilt prep workflows so the ambitious builds could run at Friday volume
  • Trained the team on the techniques the program demanded, so execution didn’t depend on any one bartender
  • Helped develop the next menu — technique development, prep workflows, and specs built to survive nightly service

What changed

  • In Plain Sight named a Spirited Awards 2023 U.S. Regional Top 10 Honoree — Best New U.S. Cocktail Bar — within twelve months
  • Coverage in The Infatuation, Austin Chronicle, and Eater Austin
  • The rooms became the place Austin bartenders go on their own nights off — the industry’s honest review

Vine Bar

New York, US · 2024 — 2025 · Menu development & team training

Vine Bar, Brooklyn. Photo: vinebk.com.

An ambitious menu that survived a full room.

The brief

A New York bar ready to push past the expected, wanting a list built on new techniques and unexpected flavors — without the usual fate of ambitious menus, which is dying quietly three weeks after launch when the prep collapses under a full room.

What we did

  • Created the Explorers menu from concept to nightly service — a highly innovative list built around new techniques and unexpected flavor pairings
  • Wrote complete recipe specs with costings, so every drink had a margin target before it had a garnish
  • Built the prep workflows that made the innovation servable — batching, clarification, and mise en place scaled to the room
  • Trained the team on every build and the techniques behind them, then trained them to train the next hire

What changed

  • The Explorers menu ran as designed — full ambition, nightly service, no quiet simplification after launch
  • Consistency held after the engagement ended: the specs, costings, and training stayed with the house
  • The bar’s program moved from “good neighborhood bar” to destination list — drinks people cross the river for

Tocayo

Nicosia, CY · 2020 — 2022 · Bar program & cocktail menu, from concept

Tocayo, Nicosia. Photo: Cesoro Group.

Best New Restaurant, with the bar to match.

The brief

One of Nicosia’s most ambitious restaurant openings — a kitchen running on technique, sourcing, and quiet confidence. The brief: a bar that could keep pace with the food, in a market where restaurant cocktails were an afterthought printed on the back of the wine list.

What we did

  • Built the beverage program from scratch: twelve drinks, every one defensible against the room
  • Paired the list to the food, not to trends — with the same seasonal turnover the chef demanded of the kitchen
  • Engineered service-volume prep from day one: every cocktail prepped, batched where integrity allowed, plated within thirty seconds in service
  • Trained the opening team and left them the spec book

What changed

  • Best New Restaurant Cyprus, 2021
  • Sustained press across Cypriot food and lifestyle media
  • A bar program that outlived the engagement — the standard the room still serves to

Casa de Cuba

Abu Dhabi, AE · 2019 · Full opening program

Atmospheric image — venue closed; no official photography available.

A rum program built for volume, in a market that doesn’t default to it.

The brief

An Abu Dhabi venue trading on a deep Caribbean catalog, in a market that doesn’t default to spirit-forward drinking and a climate that punishes delicate builds. Friday nights here mean covers NYC operators don’t typically forecast for.

What we did

  • Built the full opening program — concept, cocktail and rum menu, supplier setup, team training, and opening-week support
  • Wrote a list that reads as a single editorial: heavy on long-aged rums, smoked syrups, and tropical acids that survive the temperature differential between bar and table
  • Designed every build for service volume against an international guest base
  • Trained for speed under pressure — technique that holds at five hundred covers

What changed

  • A spirit-forward rum program that worked commercially in a market that wasn’t supposed to want one
  • Volume-ready builds — the menu’s ambition never throttled the room’s capacity
  • The full playbook — specs, prep, training — handed to the house team at close
