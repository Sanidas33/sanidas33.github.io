The hire

A lab is not a rotovap. It is a production line.

Most groups buy a piece of kit and park it next to the ice well. That is not a prep lab. A lab is the room, the load calculation, the SOPs, and the people who can run a batch without inventing food safety at 1 a.m. The public record behind this practice is a World’s 50 Best #25 program and rooms that held volume — the lab work is how that standard scales past one stick.

Spec against throughput

Rotovap, centrifuge, sous-vide, ultrasonic, Spinzall, vacuum sealer, walk-in batching, glycol chiller — only if the minutes and the menu pay for them. The itemized engagement is on Services.

Drawings the GC can use

Room layout plus electrical and plumbing requirements, delivered as architect-ready drawings. We work alongside your general contractor. We are not the contractor, and we do not pull permits.

SOPs the night team can rerun

Batching protocols, HACCP-compliant production, two-day on-site training, 30-day troubleshooting. The labor argument is in Your Labor Problem Is a Prep Problem.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What is a bar prep lab?

A dedicated production room for the work that should not happen on the stick: batching, clarification, infusions, syrups, and anything that needs HACCP-compliant process. Service bars draw from the lab. Bartenders stop doing commissary in a kitchen corner at eleven at night.

Who needs a prep-lab build versus a menu refresh?

Groups with three or more outlets, and hotels with centralized commissary potential. A single room with a prep problem usually needs a Program Refresh — workflows and batching inside the existing bar. A lab is for throughput that no longer fits behind one stick.

Do we have to buy a rotovap?

No. Equipment is specified against throughput, not a brochure. A rotovap, centrifuge, sous-vide, Spinzall, or glycol chiller that does not earn its keep in menu impact or labor minutes stays in the catalog. You buy direct from suppliers at invoice; we do not mark up kit.

What does the prep-lab engagement include — and exclude?

Site walkthrough, current-state assessment, equipment spec, architect-ready layout with electrical and plumbing requirements, batching SOPs, HACCP-compliant production protocols, two-day on-site team training, and 30-day post-launch troubleshooting. Not included: equipment purchase, general contracting, and permitting. We work alongside your GC. The itemized list is on Services.

How long does a prep-lab design and build take?

Eight to fourteen weeks, design through build supervision. Timeline moves with your GC and lead times on specified equipment — those are outside this scope.
