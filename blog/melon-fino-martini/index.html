The Melon & Fino Martini.

By Andreas Sanidiotis·July 21, 2026·6 min read

Honeydew peaks in the Northeast from mid July through September, and most bars waste it in a shaken drink where the flavor disappears. This is the opposite: clarified melon juice, acid-adjusted to wine levels, stirred into a fino-forward reverse martini at 15% ABV. It's batched, it pours from the fridge in fifteen seconds, and it's the low-proof option guests keep asking for that most menus still don't have.

The drink

Fino sherry and melon is an old pairing for a reason. Fino brings salt, almond and bruised apple. Ripe honeydew brings the sweetness fino deliberately lacks. The gin is the smallest component in the glass, there for structure and juniper lift, not the base. That inversion is what gets the drink to 15% ABV, which is the point. The commercial argument for a serious low-proof list is in the NA program note. This drink is that argument in a Nick & Nora.

Two techniques carry the spec: agar clarification and acid adjustment. Both are prep-shift work. Neither needs equipment beyond a pot, a whisk and a scale.

Finished-drink targets

  • ABV: ~15%
  • Sugar: ~25 g/L (dry, martini territory)
  • Acid: ~3.5 g/L
  • Clarity: transparent, pale green-gold. No pulp, no haze.
  • Yield: 1 L bottled = 10 serves
  • Serve: 100 ml, frozen Nick & Nora or coupe, up, no ice
  • Shelf life: 5 days refrigerated. Clarified juice freezes for later batches.

Prep 1: clarified honeydew juice

Agar clarification, the version that needs no centrifuge. You set the juice into a weak gel, break it, and let gravity pull clear liquid through the curds. One ripe honeydew around 2 kg gives roughly 800 g of juice, which clarifies down to about 680 g.

Spec, per 1,000 g strained honeydew juice

  • 2 g agar agar (0.2%)

1. Peel and seed ripe honeydew. Juice, then strain through a chinois. Yield runs ~65%, about 1.6 kg prepped flesh per 1,000 g juice. Weigh the juice, not the fruit.
2. Whisk the agar into 250 g of the juice in a small pot. Bring to a boil, hold 30 seconds, whisking. Agar only hydrates above 90°C.
3. Whisk the hot portion back into the remaining 750 g of cold juice.
4. Refrigerate 45 minutes until it sets to a soft gel.
5. Whisk the gel into curds, pour into a coffee-filter-lined chinois, and let it drain in the fridge. Do not press.
6. Yield ~850 g clear juice per 1,000 g in.

The first 100 ml may run cloudy. Pour it back through the bed. Clear in this drink is not cosmetic: pulp in a stirred, up serve settles into a ring at the bottom of the glass within a minute of hitting the pass.

Prep 2: acid adjustment

Honeydew juice has almost no acid, around a tenth of what a wine has. Poured straight into sherry it tastes flat and thick. The fix is measured, not guessed.

Spec, per 1,000 g clarified juice

  • 3 g tartaric acid
  • 3 g malic acid
  • 2 g ascorbic acid
  • 1 g fine salt

1. Whisk everything into the cold clarified juice until dissolved.
2. Taste against a splash of fino. It should read bright, not sour.
3. Refrigerate. Use within 5 days, or freeze flat in bags.

Tartaric and malic are the acids of wine and apples, so the juice reads as vinous next to the sherry instead of like lemonade. Citric would work but pushes the drink toward a gimlet. The ascorbic holds the pale green color, and the salt does what salt does to melon.

The batch

Spec, per liter of finished drink

  • 450 g fino sherry
  • 200 g London dry gin (40%)
  • 250 g clarified, acid-adjusted honeydew juice
  • 100 g filtered water

1. Combine, stir, bottle.
2. Refrigerate at 0–2°C. Scale linearly: a 5 L batch is 2.25 kg fino / 1 kg gin / 1.25 kg juice / 500 g water.
3. Rest at least 2 hours before service so it pours at fridge temperature.

One thing this bottle cannot do is live in the freezer, and the reason is worth knowing. Freezing point tracks ABV: a 40% spirit holds liquid to about −27°C, but a 15% batch freezes around −6°C, and a standard bar freezer runs −18°C. A freezer-door martini program only works at full proof. Low-proof batches hold at 0–2°C in the fridge, and the cold serve comes from the glass: keep the glassware in the freezer, not the bottle.

Service

100 ml into a frozen Nick & Nora or coupe. No ice, ever, in either glass. Garnish with a thin ribbon of honeydew on a pick, or nothing. The batch is at full dilution and fridge temperature, so the build is pour and garnish, fifteen seconds, no stirring at service.

The operator math

Ingredients run about $1.65 a serve across 10 serves per liter:

  • ~$9.00 of fino (450 g from a $15 bottle)
  • ~$5.90 of gin (200 g from a $22 bottle)
  • ~$1.50 of honeydew (one $4 melon yields juice for nearly three liters of batch)
  • Acids and salt: cents

At $16 on the menu that's a 10% pour cost on a stirred-category drink with zero à-la-minute labor. It also solves a menu problem money can't usually fix: the guest who wants a martini occasion at half the alcohol. The clarification and the acid adjustment are one prep shift, and the same clarified juice spec runs a zero-proof highball if you want the pairing: melon juice, saline, tonic.

Seasonal builds like this, with the specs, batch logs and yields written down, are what a menu refresh engagement produces. If your list has no low-proof answer beyond a spritz, fifteen minutes will tell you what it needs.
