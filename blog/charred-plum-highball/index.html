The Charred Plum Highball.

By Andreas Sanidiotis·July 24, 2026·6 min read

Plums hit the Northeast markets in late July and stay cheap through September. Char them hard, turn them into a shrub, and one prep shift gives you a bottled base that holds for eight weeks. With rye and soda it drinks like a smoked plum spritz without the smoke gun, and the acid comes from the bottle, not from a citrus station.

The drink

A shrub is fruit preserved in sugar and vinegar. It is old bar technology and it solves a modern problem: fresh stone fruit has a service life of days, a shrub has a service life of months. The char step is what makes this one worth a menu slot. Cut plums caramelized hard on a plancha pick up roasted, almost amaro-like bitterness that plays into rye spice. The vinegar reads as brightness, not sourness, once it is diluted with soda.

No citrus in the build. The whole drink is spirit, shrub, soda. That means no juicing at service, no waste at close, and a build time of about twenty seconds.

Finished-drink targets

  • ABV: ~11% in the glass
  • Serve: highball glass, clear ice spear, 165 ml liquid
  • Build: 45 ml rye, 30 ml shrub, 90 ml soda
  • Color: deep ruby, transparent. Coffee filter is not optional.
  • Shrub shelf life: 8 weeks refrigerated
  • Cost: ~$1.60 a serve, ~11% pour cost at $15

Prep: charred plum shrub

Everything is weighed. One kilo of pitted plums is roughly 9 medium plums, about 1.1 kg whole fruit. Ripe to slightly overripe is correct: this is also the spec for the fruit your kitchen is about to throw away.

Spec, per 1,000 g pitted plum halves

  • 600 g white sugar
  • 350 g apple cider vinegar (5% acidity)
  • 100 g filtered water
  • 2 g fine salt

1. Halve and pit ripe red or black plums. Weigh after pitting.
2. Char cut side down on a dry plancha or cast iron pan, high heat, 3 to 4 minutes. You want deep brown to black edges on the cut face. No oil.
3. Rough-chop the charred halves, skin on. Combine with the sugar and salt in a nonreactive container, mix, cover, and refrigerate 24 hours. The sugar pulls the juice out.
4. Add the vinegar and water, stir until the remaining sugar dissolves, and refrigerate another 24 hours.
5. Strain through a chinois, pressing the solids, then pass once through a coffee filter.
6. Yield: ~1,550 g, about 1.25 L. Bottle, label, date. Holds 8 weeks at 0–4°C.

The coffee filter matters because the serve is topped with soda. Fine particles are nucleation sites: a hazy shrub foams on the pour and goes flat in the glass. A clean shrub pours bright and holds its bubble. Same rule as any carbonated serve.

The 48 hours are passive. Active prep time for a 3 kg run is about 40 minutes, and 3 kg of plums gives you just under 4 liters of shrub, enough for roughly 125 drinks.

The batch

For speed of service, premix spirit and shrub. Soda stays separate until the glass.

Spec, per liter of premix

  • 570 g rye whiskey (600 ml at 40–45%)
  • 460 g charred plum shrub (400 ml)

1. Combine, stir, bottle.
2. Refrigerate. The premix is shelf-stable for the life of the shrub. One liter is 13 serves at 75 ml.
3. At service: 75 ml premix over a clear ice spear, top with 90 ml cold soda, one gentle lift with a barspoon. Garnish with a charred plum wedge on a pick, or nothing.

Rye at 45% stands up better to the vinegar than at 40. If the list already carries a bonded rye, use it here. The same premix logic runs a zero-proof version: shrub and soda alone at 30/120 is a legitimate NA listing, and it costs about 30 cents.

The operator math

Per serve, at retail-ish prices:

  • Rye: 45 ml from a $22 bottle, ~$1.32
  • Shrub: 30 ml, ~$0.20 (plums at $3/lb, sugar and vinegar cents)
  • Soda: ~$0.10
  • Total: ~$1.60. At $15 on the menu, ~11% pour cost.

The quieter number is waste. Stone fruit that softens past salad quality is exactly what the char step wants, so the shrub runs on fruit you were already paying for. And because the base holds 8 weeks, this is one of the few seasonal drinks you can still be selling in mid September from a batch made in July.

Why this technique earns its place

Shrubs get treated as a farmers-market gimmick. Costed properly they are a preservation system: one 40-minute prep block converts perishable fruit into two months of acid-balanced, bar-ready product with no juicer, no clarification rig, and no special equipment beyond a pan and a scale. Swap the fruit and the same ratios carry you through the year. Peaches now, concord grapes in September, cranberries in November.

Builds like this, written down with yields, shelf life and costs, are what a menu refresh engagement produces. If your seasonal drinks die when the fruit does, fifteen minutes will tell you what to fix.
