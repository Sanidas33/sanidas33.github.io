Sweet Corn Milk Punch.

By Andreas Sanidiotis·July 17, 2026·6 min read

Sweet corn is at its best in New York from mid July through September, and this punch uses the whole ear. The kernels become a warm corn syrup, the cobs and husks flavor the bourbon, and a small dose of ancho chile liqueur sits underneath everything. The batch is clarified through milk, keeps four weeks in the fridge, and service is pour, cube, nutmeg.

The drink

Milk clarification does three jobs here. It strips out the haze, it softens the lemon so the punch drinks smooth at 18% ABV, and it stabilizes the batch so it lasts. If you want the technique background, it's in the milk wash note. This is that technique doing paid work.

The ancho liqueur is there for depth, not heat. At 5 ml per drink you get a little warmth and a raisin note that sits well under cooked corn and bourbon. You taste it most in the finish.

Finished-drink targets

  • ABV: ~18%
  • Sugar: ~95 g/L
  • Acid: ~7 g/L
  • Clarity: transparent, pale gold. No haze.
  • Yield: ~1.8 L (18 serves)
  • Serve: 100 ml, rocks glass, one large clear cube, grated nutmeg
  • Shelf life: 4 weeks refrigerated. Better after 48 hours.

Prep 1: toasted cob and husk bourbon

Buy five ears. Everything below comes from them.

Spec, per 750 ml bourbon (45%)

  • Cobs from 5 ears, kernels cut off (~350 g)
  • Husks from 5 ears, silk removed (~40 g)

1. Quarter the cobs. Spread cobs and husks on a tray.
2. Toast at 180°C for 15 minutes, until golden with light char at the edges.
3. Combine hot with the bourbon in a bag or jar. Sous vide 55°C for 90 minutes, or 12 hours at room temperature.
4. Strain through a coffee filter. Rebottle, label, date.

The infusion reads as popcorn and dry grass. It keeps like bourbon.

Prep 2: warm corn syrup

Juice the kernels raw, then cook the juice gently with the sugar. Twenty minutes at 60°C gives you cooked corn flavor, closer to corn on the grill than raw kernels, without setting the starch. Stay at 60. Much hotter and corn juice starts turning into polenta.

Spec

  • Kernels from 5 ears (~550 g), juiced raw. Target 400 g juice. No juicer: blend with 100 g water for 30 seconds and press through a fine bag.
  • 130 g white sugar
  • 2 g fine salt

1. Thermomix: 60°C, 20 minutes, speed 2. No Thermomix: a sealed bag at 60°C in the sous vide bath for 30 minutes, or the lowest flame with a thermometer, stirring.
2. Fine-strain.
3. Yield ~500 g. Keeps a week refrigerated.

The punch

Standard milk wash direction applies: pour the punch into the milk, never the milk into the punch. Milk poured into acid splits into fine grains that never knit into a curd bed, and then the filter clogs.

Spec, one batch (~1.8 L finished)

  • 650 g toasted cob and husk bourbon
  • 85 g ancho chile liqueur (that's 5 ml per serve across the batch)
  • Corn syrup from above (~500 g, all of it)
  • 200 g fresh lemon juice
  • 250 g filtered water
  • 330 g cold whole milk, in a separate container

1. Combine everything except the milk. This is the punch.
2. Pour the punch into the cold milk in a steady stream, stirring once or twice. It curdles immediately. Stop stirring.
3. Rest 2 hours at room temperature, or overnight refrigerated.
4. Rack through a coffee-filter-lined chinois. The curd bed is the real filter. Pour the first cloudy 200 ml back through the bed until it runs clear.
5. Bottle, label, refrigerate.

Scale linearly. A 5x batch is ~9 L, about 90 serves.

The first pass is slow, an hour or more for a full batch. Don't squeeze or press the curd. Pressure pushes fines through and you end up filtering twice. Set it up at the start of the prep shift and let gravity work while you do everything else.

Clarity is the spec, not a preference. A hazy milk punch reads as a mistake at the pass, and the suspended solids are what shorten shelf life. If it pours cloudy, it goes back through the curd.

Service

100 ml over one large clear cube in a rocks glass. Grate nutmeg over the top. Corn, ancho and nutmeg belong together. Nothing else in the glass. The drink is batched at full dilution, so the service build takes fifteen seconds.

The operator math

Ingredients run about $1.50 a serve across 18 serves:

  • ~$15 of bourbon
  • ~$4 of ancho chile liqueur
  • ~$5 of corn, used entirely: kernels in the syrup, cobs and husks in the bourbon
  • ~$3 of lemons, sugar and milk

At $16 on the menu that's a 9% pour cost. The scheduling math matters more. Every fresh-juice drink on a menu is a daily prep obligation. This one is monthly. One batch made in one shift covers four weeks of service with no à-la-minute labor beyond ice and nutmeg. It's the argument from the prep systems note, applied to one glass.
