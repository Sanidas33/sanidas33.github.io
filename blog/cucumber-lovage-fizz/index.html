The Cucumber & Lovage Fizz.

By Andreas Sanidiotis·August 17, 2026·5 min read

August cucumbers cost about a dollar a pound and most bars only ever slice them into a garnish. This drink uses the whole fruit, split in two: the peels infuse the gin, the flesh goes under sugar with fresh lovage for a cold oleo-saccharum. A yogurt foam from the siphon does the job egg white usually does, with none of the service problems. One kilo of cucumbers, two preps, nothing in the bin.

The drink

Cucumber drinks usually mean muddled slices and a watery result. Splitting the fruit fixes that. The aromatics live in the skin, so that is what goes into the spirit. The flesh is mostly water and freshness, so sugar pulls it into a syrup cold, the way an oleo-saccharum pulls oil from citrus peel. Lovage goes into the oleo with it: it reads as celery with more intent, and it is what keeps the drink savory instead of spa-water sweet.

The foam is yogurt, not egg. It brings its own acidity and a lactic edge that sits well against the cucumber, it holds better on a fizz, and it takes the raw-egg conversation off the menu entirely.

Finished-drink targets

ABV: ~13%
Serve: highball, ~140 ml plus foam
Look: pale green, clear under a white foam cap
Garnish: none. The foam is the garnish

Prep 1: cucumber-peel gin

Spec, per 750 ml London dry gin (40%)

Peels from 1,000 g cucumbers (about 80–100 g)

1. Wash the cucumbers, peel them, keep the flesh for the oleo below.
2. Add the peels to the gin. 24 hours at room temperature.
3. Strain, bottle, label, date.

Yield ~750 ml, 16 serves. Spirit-stable; the oleo is the clock in this drink.

Prep 2: cucumber & lovage oleo

No heat anywhere. The sugar draws the juice out of the cut flesh overnight and dissolves into it. What comes off the strainer is a finished syrup that tastes like the whole cucumber, not a cooked one.

Spec, per 1,000 g peeled cucumber

750 g caster sugar
20 g fresh lovage, chopped

1. Cut the peeled cucumbers into rough dice.
2. Chop the lovage. Mix cucumber, sugar and lovage in a container until everything is coated. Mix it properly; dry pockets of sugar slow the draw.
3. Overnight in the fridge.
4. Strain, pressing gently. Bottle, label, date.

Yield ~1 L, about 66 serves at 15 ml. 15 days refrigerated.
One oleo batch outlasts four bottles of the peel gin; scale the infusion, not the syrup.

Prep 3: yogurt foam

Spec, per 0.5 L siphon

500 g plain whole-milk yogurt
60 g caster sugar
2 g fine salt
Grated zest of one lemon

1. Whisk everything together until smooth.
2. Into the siphon, 2 N2O charges, shake well.
3. Fridge at least 30 minutes before service.

About 25 serves. 3 days refrigerated in the siphon.

The build

Spec, per serve

45 ml cucumber-peel gin
20 ml fresh lemon juice
15 ml cucumber & lovage oleo
45 ml soda

1. Shake the gin, lemon and oleo hard with ice.
2. Strain into a highball over cubed ice.
3. Top with the soda, lift once with the barspoon.
4. Foam on top from the siphon. No garnish.

There is no service batch on this one. The oleo lives strained and bottled in the fridge door, the gin is on the rail, and the build is a standard shake. Where the time was spent is the prep list, which is where it belongs.

The operator math

Ingredient cost per serve, using a $20 well London dry:

  • Peel gin, 45 ml: ~$1.20 (the peels are free; they came off the oleo cucumbers)
  • Oleo, 15 ml: ~$0.07 (1 kg cucumbers at ~$1/lb, 750 g sugar and a handful of lovage makes 66 serves)
  • Lemon, 20 ml: ~$0.22
  • Soda: ~$0.08
  • Yogurt foam: ~$0.18 a serve including the charges

Call it $1.75 a serve. At $15 on the menu that is a 12% pour cost. The whole drink is built from a well gin, a supermarket dairy aisle, and produce at its cheapest week of the year. Drop the gin and the oleo works with lemon, soda and the foam as an NA listing that costs about $0.55 to put in the glass.

Same argument as the watermelon and plum notes: the fruit is split so nothing is bought twice and nothing is thrown away, and the spec is written so any bartender on the roster can run it. The skill is in the prep sheet, not the service.
