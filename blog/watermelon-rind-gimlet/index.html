The Watermelon Rind Gimlet.

By Andreas Sanidiotis·August 7, 2026·6 min read

August watermelon is the cheapest fruit at the market, and a third of it is rind that goes in the bin. This drink uses the whole melon. The flesh becomes a clarified, acid-adjusted cordial. The rind becomes a quick pickle. The pickle brine goes into the build, and the pickle itself is the garnish. Two preps, one stir, served up.

The drink

A gimlet is gin and cordial. Watermelon on its own is too polite for the job: mostly water and sugar, almost no acid. So the cordial gets adjusted up to citrus strength, and the pickle brine brings a second kind of acidity, acetic, with coriander and white pepper riding along. The result pours clear and pale coral, hits like a lime gimlet, and finishes savory instead of sweet.

It is also the first pickle on this blog. The technique costs a pot of brine and 24 hours of waiting, runs on trim you already paid for, and the same ratio works on cucumber ends, fennel stalks, and unripe stone fruit. Worth learning once.

Finished-drink targets

ABV: ~20%
Sugar: ~95 g/L
Acid: ~9 g/L, citric, malic, and acetic together
Clarity: clear, pale coral. Settle and coffee-filter the juice
Serve: ~115 ml, chilled Nick & Nora or small coupe, no ice

Prep 1: pickled rind and brine

Peel the green skin off with a Y-peeler and keep only the white part. It pickles crisp, like a firmer cucumber, and it drinks the brine flavor overnight.

Spec, per 1,000 g trimmed white rind

400 g white wine vinegar, 5% acidity
400 g water
200 g white sugar
30 g fine sea salt
6 g coriander seed
2 g white peppercorn

1. Peel off all the green skin. Cut the white rind into 1 cm dice.
2. Bring vinegar, water, sugar, salt, and spices to a boil.
3. Pour the hot brine over the rind in a sanitized container. Cool to room temperature, lid, refrigerate.
4. Ready in 24 hours. Strain what you need at service; keep rind and brine together in storage.

Yield ~1 kg pickled rind plus ~950 ml brine. Three weeks refrigerated.

Prep 2: watermelon cordial

Cold process, same family as the green tomato and peach cordials. Watermelon juices almost by itself, but it comes off the blender cloudy and it browns fast. Settling, filtering, and a small ascorbic dose fix both.

Spec, per 1,000 g clarified juice

450 g white sugar
22 g citric acid
8 g malic acid
3 g ascorbic acid
2 g fine salt

1. Cube the flesh, blitz, pass through a chinois. About 1.3 kg flesh gives 1,000 g clear juice once settling losses are in.
2. Settle 12 hours in the fridge. Rack the clear juice off the solids, then pass it through a coffee filter.
3. Whisk in the dry ingredients cold until dissolved. Do not heat.

Yield ~1.29 L, about 43 serves. Two weeks refrigerated; freeze what week one does not use.

The adjustment logic: watermelon juice arrives at roughly 2 g/L of acid, nowhere near cordial strength. Citric brings the brightness, malic gives the fresh-fruit snap, ascorbic holds the pink against oxidation, and the salt makes the melon taste more like itself. The acetic side stays out of the cordial on purpose; it comes in per serve from the brine, so you can tune it drink by drink.

The build

Spec, per serve

60 ml London dry gin
30 ml watermelon cordial
7.5 ml pickle brine, strained

1. Stir over ice ~30 seconds to 20–25% dilution.
2. Fine-strain into a chilled Nick & Nora or small coupe.
3. Garnish: one cube of pickled rind on a pick, laid across the rim. Nothing else.

Freezer batch, per liter

510 ml London dry gin
255 ml watermelon cordial
65 ml pickle brine, strained
170 ml filtered water

1. Combine, bottle, freeze at −18°C. The water is the stir dilution, measured instead of guessed.
2. Service: pour ~115 ml into a chilled glass, garnish. Done in ten seconds.

8 serves per liter. Holds 2 weeks frozen; the clock is the cordial’s.

Served up means no ice in the glass, ever. Cold comes from the stir or the freezer, and the chilled glass keeps it there.

The operator math

One 4.5 kg melon at ~$6 gives about 2 kg of clarified juice, two cordial batches, roughly 85 serves, plus a kilo of trimmed rind: garnish for all of them and more brine than the batches will use. Ingredient cost per serve, using a $20 well London dry:

  • Gin, 60 ml: ~$1.60
  • Cordial, 30 ml: ~$0.15 (the melon’s share plus sugar and acids, 43 serves a batch)
  • Brine, 7.5 ml: ~$0.03 (vinegar and sugar over trim you already own)

Call it $1.80 a serve. At $16 on the menu that is an 11% pour cost, on a drink that takes ten seconds to serve from the freezer batch. The cordial with 10 ml of brine and soda over ice makes an NA listing at ~$0.25 in the glass.

Same point as the tepache note: the waste stream is the ingredient. The melon was already on the prep list for the cordial; the rind just stopped being garbage.
