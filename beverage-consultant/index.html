What you’re hiring

Not a menu. A machine.

Most beverage consultants hand you recipes. This practice builds the operation behind them — because a list without costings, prep systems, and a trained team is homework, not consulting.

The program

Concept, cocktail menu, wine and spirits curation, and NA list — designed to your food, room, and guest, with every recipe costed against a target pour cost. Opening or refresh.

The economics

Batching, prep workflows, supplier setup, and inventory discipline that pull pour costs into the healthy band — the margin work typically pays the consulting fee. How pour cost gets fixed.

The team

In-person training, spec books, and train-the-trainer so the program survives staff changes. You own every document when the engagement ends.

How it works

The room first. The menu second.

The work starts with the room you have, not a menu borrowed from another city. The first call covers the concept, the kitchen, the hours, and what is failing — a list guests skip, a pour cost that drifted, a team that cannot make the same drink twice. From that you get a fixed scope: what is included, how many weeks it takes, and whether wine, a non-alcoholic list, or days on the floor are in. Nothing starts until that scope is the one you agreed to.

Recipes are written in the measures the bar will pour, then costed. Prep is designed for a busy shift, not a photograph. Training happens in the room, and the specs, costings, and prep notes go to the house. If the station is the constraint, the setup is in how to set up a cocktail prep lab; a designed room is prep-lab design. A hotel, with more than one outlet and more than one daypart, is not a longer version of this page — that brief is hotel bar consultant.

What you leave with

Documents the next hire can run.

A cocktail list written to the guest and the speed of the well, plus a wine, spirits, or non-alcoholic edit where the room needs it. Every spec has a costing — the method is the pour cost guide — and prep notes that say what is made ahead, how it is stored, and how long it keeps. The senior bartender can rerun the training. Which engagement those pieces belong to is on services.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What does a beverage consultant actually do?

A beverage consultant designs and fixes the drinks side of a hospitality business: menu development with costed recipes, prep systems and batching, supplier negotiation, pour-cost management, and team training. The deliverable is a program your team runs profitably without the consultant on payroll.

Do you work outside New York?

Yes. The practice is based in New York and built for anywhere — the portfolio spans Austin, Nicosia, and Abu Dhabi. Projects outside NYC run on intensive on-site blocks with remote development between them.

How is a beverage consultant priced?

Fixed project fees scoped before work begins — from menu refreshes to full opening programs — or a monthly advisory retainer. You'll know the full cost up front; every recipe, costing, and SOP is yours at the end.

When should we bring a beverage consultant in?

For openings: twelve to sixteen weeks before doors. For running venues: the moment margins drift or the menu stops earning attention. Earlier is always cheaper than later.

What is the difference between a beverage consultant and a bar consultant?

A beverage consultant owns the drinks program as a whole: cocktails, and where the room needs them, wine, spirits, and non-alcoholic. A bar consultant is the operations hire — pour cost, prep, suppliers, and the way the stick runs. Many rooms need both. The operations page is bar consultant. This page is the program.

Do you design hotel beverage programs?

Hotel openings and running hotel bars are scoped on their own page, because a property has more than one place to pour and more than one daypart. Start with hotel bar consultant. This page stays with restaurants, bars, and drinks programs.

What do we own when the engagement ends?

Every recipe, costing sheet, prep note, and training document. The bar should not depend on the consultant after the consultant leaves. If a proposal says the specs stay with the consultant, it is the wrong proposal.

How long does a beverage program take?

A refresh of an existing list is typically six to ten weeks. An opening wants twelve to sixteen weeks before doors, plus time after opening to see what service does to the menu. Tighter timelines are possible. They are a different scope, and the call is where that gets said.
