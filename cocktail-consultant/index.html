How a menu gets built

A cocktail menu development process.

Read the room before writing a drink: food, dayparts, glassware, the well, and how many hands are on the stick at eight. A short list follows. Ten to eighteen drinks is the usual refresh; a full opening list runs longer and is still cut until every drink has a reason to be there. Spec and cost each one before it is printed, in the measures the bar will pour. If it cannot be repeated on a Saturday, it does not ship.

Prep is only what that list needs, with a shelf life and a label. The station version is how to set up a cocktail prep lab; a separate room is prep-lab design. Train the people who will make the drinks, including the closer who missed the first session, and hand over the book. A hotel list — lobby hour and banquet in one program — starts on hotel bar consultant.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What's the difference between a mixologist and a cocktail consultant?

A mixologist designs and makes drinks. A cocktail consultant delivers the drinks and the system around them — specs, costings, prep workflows, and training — so the menu runs consistently at volume without its author behind the bar. This practice does both, and always ships the system.

Can you develop signature cocktails for our venue or brand?

Yes — signature menus for venues, and signature serves for spirits brands, including trade education and bartender training so the serve actually gets made in the field. See Brand Advocacy for brand-side work.

Do you run cocktail masterclasses and team training?

Yes. Technique masterclasses (from Japanese stirring to rotovap distillation), account-level trainings for brands, and full opening-team training for venues — designed to be rerun by your own people.

How many drinks are in a typical menu you develop?

Ten to eighteen for a refresh. A full opening list runs longer, and it is still a short list with a point of view rather than an encyclopedia. A hotel program, with more than one outlet, is scoped on the hotel bar consultant page. Every drink ships with a costed spec.

How is cocktail consulting priced?

As a fixed project fee, scoped before any work begins, or as a monthly advisory retainer when you want ongoing eyes on a list the house already runs. The figure is in the proposal, not on this page. Every spec, costing, and prep note is yours at the end.

How are cocktail menu fees scoped?

By the work, before it starts. A short refresh, a full opening list, the number of training days, and whether wine or a prep system is included are different scopes. This page does not list fees. What operators actually pay, and what moves a quote, is explained in How much does a bar consultant cost? You receive a fixed figure in a proposal after a call, and you own the specs when the work ends.

Who is a cocktail menu consultant for?

Bars rewriting a stale list, restaurants whose drinks lag the kitchen, and spirits brands that need a serve a working bartender will actually make. It is a poor fit for a single signature drink with no spec and no training. Hotel menus, with lobby, restaurant, and banquet in one program, start on the hotel bar consultant page.

What do you hand over with the menu?

A spec for every drink, a costing, the prep that drink requires, and a training session the team can rerun. The house owns the documents. A menu card without those pieces is a list of ideas, not a menu the closer can make.
